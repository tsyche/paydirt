## Phase 4 — Allowance / Payments

- Configurable reward types: in-app currency, Venmo, bank transfer, manual — all with in-app record-keeping
  - 🧑 needs-human: Venmo/bank integrations need real accounts and credentials. The in-app-currency and manual record-keeping paths are agent-doable on their own
- **Google Wallet kids balance (idea, 2026-08-26)** — "Wallet for kids" (per Google's own in-app description): parents set up a balance for a kid, kid gets their own tap-to-pay payment card, parent gets transaction notifications + remote spending controls + can remove the payment method remotely. Kid can't check out online or "across Google" (Wallet-only, no browser/app checkout) — tap-to-pay in physical stores only. Requires an "eligible device" (unconfirmed what that means for GrapheneOS/LineageOS — likely a Play Services / SafetyNet-style requirement, which is exactly what this stack avoids everywhere else). At 13 the kid can opt to convert it to a self-managed Google Account, so this is explicitly a bridge feature, not a permanent arrangement. Idea: a parentBucks payout option that converts virtual currency to a real-money top-up of the kid's Wallet balance, at a configurable conversion rate — same shape as the existing "more screen time" manual-grant payout, but real money instead. Not yet scoped; needs a feasibility spike first (does topping up the balance have an API, or is it manual-only through the Google Wallet app? fees? does it actually work on a degoogled device, or does that kill this path immediately? what happens to the balance/history at the 13 handoff?) before any design or implementation work.
  - 🧑 needs-human: requires a Google account with the Wallet kids feature (and a kid device to test "eligible device" against) to research feasibility; conflicts with the project's no-Google-Play-Services stance (see *Key Concepts* in [AGENTS.md](./AGENTS.md)) and needs a decision on whether that tradeoff is acceptable for this one payout path
- **Reloadable prepaid card (idea, 2026-08-26)** — alternative to the Google Wallet angle above: a physical reloadable prepaid card (e.g. Greenlight, GoHenry, or a plain reloadable Visa/Mastercard gift card product) instead of tying the payout to a specific Google account or device. Same parentBucks-to-real-money conversion idea, but the parent reloads the card's balance instead of a Wallet balance. Upsides worth weighing against the Wallet route: no Google/Play-Services dependency at all (so it sidesteps the "eligible device" question entirely), works with any physical card the kid carries, and a lost/damaged card is just a card — cancel and reissue, no device or account entanglement. Tradeoffs: probably needs a real card-issuing platform (Greenlight/GoHenry are kid-focused and likely have their own app + spending controls, which may duplicate what PayDirt already does; a generic reloadable gift card may not support programmatic reloads at all). Needs its own feasibility spike: which providers support API-driven reloads vs. manual-only, monthly/per-card fees, minimum age, and whether a provider's own parental-control app would compete with or complement PayDirt's.
  - **Current lean (2026-08-26): plain reloadable gift card, minimal fees, not a full kid-banking service like Greenlight/GoHenry** — avoids paying for/running a parallel parental-controls app that duplicates PayDirt. Main open concern is doing the reload itself safely in an automated way (unattended card-reload API/flow, without hardcoding funding-source credentials in `pb_hooks` or anywhere else in the repo) — that's the crux of the feasibility spike, more than provider selection.
  - 🧑 needs-human: requires researching/signing up for a real card-issuing provider (fees, KYC, likely a real bank/funding account) to evaluate feasibility

## Backlog (unscheduled)

Ordered by impact-to-effort (2026-11-01 audit). Shipped items live in the ledger, not here.

- **Kid "ask for more time"** — per-chore pay mode shipped 2026-10-31 (see the shipped ledger). Still deferred: the kid ask button with a parent reply sheet that offers a bonus chore, suggested chores/minutes, parent-triggered nudges, and a daily bonus-minutes cap. Rejected for now: kid picks bucks or time, staleness premiums, automatic nudges. Full design: [docs/plans/bonus-minutes-and-time-asks.md](docs/plans/bonus-minutes-and-time-asks.md).
- **Chore notes / extra details** — optional free-form text on each chore for instructions or context ("rinse first, load top rack", "photo of the finished shelf"). `chores.description` (and the same field on `chore_templates`) already exists in the schema and types but no create/edit form sets it and no kid or approval view shows it, so this is mostly UI: add a multiline field to the web and mobile chore create/edit forms and template pre-fill, show it on the kid chore card (a short expandable line in simplified mode, not a wall of text) and in the approval queue row, and cap the length server-side. No migration expected. Open question: whether to snapshot the note onto the assignment so editing a chore later doesn't rewrite what an in-flight assignment showed. ~2-3 hrs. **Gotchas:** render as plain text only (no HTML or markdown) so a note can't inject markup; notes are visible to the kids, so keep parent-only comments out of this field (a separate parent-only note would be a different feature); templates copy the note at create time, so later template edits won't update existing chores; if the note is snapshotted onto the assignment, old assignments keep stale text; long notes will wreck the simplified-mode card layout, so truncate with expand.
- **Recurring due days** — a due day/time per cadence chore, e.g. weekly bins "done by Tuesday 8pm" for Wednesday pickup. Today `chores.due_at` is a single fixed timestamp (reminders escalate 24h → 2h → overdue in `scheduler.js`), so it can't repeat. Add a recurring due rule (weekday + time for weekly, time for daily, day-of-month for monthly) and have cron auto-assignment stamp each new assignment with its own computed deadline, so the existing escalation and overdue pings work per cycle. Show "due Tue" on the kid card and approval row. Open questions: whether an overdue chore stays open, expires, or pays reduced; and weekday handling across timezones (the household is single-timezone, so use its local time). ~3-5 hrs. **Gotchas:** weekday and time must use the household's local time, not UTC; `isQuietHours` already uses server-local time, so pick one convention and keep it everywhere, and watch the DST changeovers; cron auto-assignment only recreates a chore when no open one exists, so an overdue chore blocks the next cycle and the deadline never rolls; an assignment created after this cycle's due time must target the next occurrence, not an already-passed one; every overdue ping goes to parents, so a chronically late weekly chore will spam them each cycle unless the pings are capped; vacation mode must pause the deadlines too; keep the existing one-off `due_at` working alongside the new rule.
- **Team chores (do it together)** — a chore kids do jointly, like helping cook dinner, where each participating kid earns the full reward (the opposite of `race`, where only the first approval pays). A non-race chore assigned to several kids already pays each one separately, but they complete and get approved separately, so a parent approves twice for one shared effort. Add a `team` flag that groups the assignments: either kid marks it done for the group (or each confirms), the parent approves once, and every member's assignment pays in the same step. Reuse the race hook's sibling-assignment lookup in `races.pb.js`. Open questions: whether one kid skipping drops only their share, whether the reward splits or stays full per kid (full here), and how a team chore counts against `max_per_period` and streaks (once per kid). `team` and `race` should be mutually exclusive in the forms. ~4-6 hrs. **Gotchas:** a team chore pays every member in full, so it doubles the cost the budget assumed (the ~$91/month worst case treats chores as independent); an approved time grant can't be undone, so undo for a team approval must either be blocked outright or reverse every member's payout together, never half; one kid who never confirms can block the group, so the parent needs a drop-a-member action; the race hook's sibling-assignment lookup keys on race semantics and must not close teammates' assignments; per-kid limits and streaks count once per member; old APKs won't know the flag, so confirm the app handles an unknown group status.
- **Per-kid weekly potential and fairness view** — a forward-looking summary per kid of what their assigned chores could earn in a week, so parents can check the load is fair. Today the web Activity report and the weekly recap only look back at what was done and earned. Compute from active assignments and recurring chores: occurrences per week from cadence and `max_per_period`, times reward, split into bucks and bonus minutes (`pay_mode`), plus a dollar and screen-minute equivalent via `goods_rate` and `screen_rate`. Show a side-by-side card per kid (chore count, max weekly bucks, minutes, $ equivalent) with an effort-adjusted comparison, since the ages differ (6 vs 11) and equal totals aren't necessarily fair. Add "actual vs potential" over the last few weeks to show who's capturing how much. Pure shared function (unit-testable) feeding a web dashboard card and a mobile parent card. Open questions: whether to weight by estimated effort or age, and whether team and race chores count at full value. ~4-6 hrs. **Gotchas:** the potential is theoretical, because `max_per_period` limits are advisory and client-side only, so the number overstates what kids can actually claim and understates what they could game; one-off chores and unscheduled work don't fit a weekly figure; mixing bucks, minutes and dollars into one number depends on `screen_rate` and `goods_rate`, so the card must say which rates it used and change when they change; a side-by-side comparison can start sibling rivalry, so keep it parent-only, never on kid screens; effort or age weighting is subjective and will read as a verdict, so show raw numbers first and label any adjustment as a guide.
- **Activity heatmap (year-at-a-glance)** (2026-11-01) — a GitHub-style contribution calendar: one small box per day for the last ~52 weeks, shaded by approved chore count that day, with a hover/tap tooltip ("Tue 3 Nov: 4 chores, +12 bucks"). Sits beside the existing Activity report (monthly numbers) as the visual trend: streaks, dry spells, and busy weeks show up without reading a table. Data already exists (approved `assignments.approved_at`, plus `currency_transactions` for the tooltip's bucks), so no schema change.
  - **Decided:** parents see one household grid (all kids combined); kids see only their own. Intensity is chore count, not bucks, so a big one-off doesn't wash out the scale. Calm and simple modes get a short "this month" strip instead of the full year.
  - **Build:** a pure shared function (`bucketByDay(rows, range)` plus a 0-4 intensity scale, unit-tested like `summarizeMonthActivity`) and a thin renderer per platform: CSS grid on web, `View` grid on mobile. Parity rule applies to the parent view (web and mobile together); the kid view is mobile-only. ~4-6 hrs.
  - **Later, maybe:** a per-kid filter on the parent grid.
- **Partial credit** — let a parent approve a bigger chore at a fraction of its reward when the kid did part of it, like folding half the socks before running out of steam. Today approval is all-or-nothing (approve pays the full reward, reject sends it back). Add an approve-partial action on the approval row (quick picks like 25/50/75% plus a custom amount) that pays the scaled bucks and minutes (minutes rounded to the 5-minute step), records the percentage on the assignment for the ledger and history, and notes it to the kid kindly ("Half done, paid half, nice effort"). Optionally a per-chore flag marks which chores allow partial credit so small ones stay all-or-nothing. A kid-side "I did part of it" marker is a later option. Open questions: whether the remainder stays open for finishing later, and how a partial counts toward streaks, badges and `max_per_period` (counts as done). Time grants are irreversible, so the confirm must show the scaled minutes. ~3-5 hrs. **Gotchas:** the ledger is append-only, so a partial approval must write one earn row at the scaled amount, never approve in full and then adjust down; undo must reverse that exact net; the pay math exists twice (`lib/chorePay.js` and `packages/shared/src/chorePay.ts`) and the scaling has to land in both with a parity test; minutes rounded to the 5-minute step can round a small partial to zero, so define the floor; kids may learn to stop early expecting half, so partial credit should stay a parent-only decision, with a kid marker later if ever; time grants are irreversible, so the confirm must show the scaled minutes; partial interacts with race (does the winner's partial still close the others?) and team chores (per-member percentages?).
- **Kid "My Bank" dashboard** — a small kid-facing dashboard, parallel to the parent dashboard, that shows at minimum the kid's bank total in both dollars (`balance / goods_rate`) and potential screen time (`balance / screen_rate` minutes, floored to the 5-minute step), updating in real time as chores are approved, purchases happen and streak bonuses land. Today `KidHome` shows the balance and a dollar value, and `SimpleKidHome` shows the same hero card, but neither shows the screen-minute equivalent at a glance, and the dollar figure is hidden when `goods_rate` is 0. Build a shared pure function (`bankView(balance, goodsRate, screenRate)`) so both kid screens and a parent view of each kid use the same math, then render it as a compact card on both kid homes (big numbers and icons in simplified mode). Reuse the existing `subscribeToKidUpdates` realtime hook and `useCountUp`. Optional extras: pending/awaiting-approval bucks as a "coming soon" line, progress toward the next goal, and a streak counter. ~3-4 hrs. **Gotchas:** the screen-minute figure is potential only, because the ledger doesn't reserve it and a request still needs parent approval, so label it "could buy" rather than "you have"; `screen_rate` and `goods_rate` change household-wide and a kid's own `goods_rate` can override, so recompute live instead of caching and use the same `me.goods_rate ?? household.goods_rate` fallback as today; the balance is a cache of `currency_transactions`, so don't derive it client-side from events; realtime re-fetches on the kid's records but misses household rate edits unless the household record is also subscribed; showing dollars next to minutes invites kids to compare prices, so keep wording neutral; test the rounding at the edges (balance below one 5-minute block, rate 0).
